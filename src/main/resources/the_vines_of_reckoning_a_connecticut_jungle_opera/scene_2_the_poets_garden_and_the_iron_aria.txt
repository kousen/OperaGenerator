Scene 2: The Poet's Garden and the Iron Aria
Author: Claude Opus 4.5

*The curtain rises on a hidden clearing deep in the jungle--SYLVAN's sanctuary. It is a place of astonishing beauty and mad invention. He has cultivated a garden in the ruins of a collapsed shopping mall, its skylights shattered and open to the canopy above. Vines have been trained into archways. Salvaged mirrors redirect sunlight into warm pools where orchids bloom in impossible profusion. Along the walls, hundreds of bark-paper sonnets are pinned, fluttering like moth wings in the humid breeze.*

*Center stage: a grand piano--half-consumed by a fig tree, its keys yellowed but still functional where the roots have not yet reached. The tree's trunk grows through the instrument's body, and when the wind moves through the leaves, the piano's strings murmur sympathetically, producing ghost chords.*

*SYLVAN leads MIRABEL into this space. She stops, stunned.*

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
> This is impossible.<br>
> A garden in a shopping mall.<br>
> A piano married to a tree.<br>
> You've built a world inside the wreckage.<br>

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
> Not built. Negotiated.<br>
> The jungle wanted to reclaim this space.<br>
> I wanted to keep it.<br>
> We compromised.<br>

*(He gestures to where vines and human craftsmanship interweave.)*
> The fig tree gets the bass notes.<br>
> I get the treble.<br>
> We share the middle register<br>
> and argue about tempo.<br>

*MIRABEL laughs--perhaps for the first time in weeks. She sets down her map case and moves through the garden, touching sonnets pinned to the walls, reading fragments.*

**MIRABEL** (soprano, reading):
"To the Aetna Diner, whose neon ghost still flickers
in the dreams of every short-order philosopher..."

"Ode to the Bushnell, whose stage now hosts
a company of tree frogs performing Ionesco..."

*(She turns to him.)*
You've written love letters to every building in Hartford.

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
Every building I could find.
Some are underwater. Some are underground.
Some exist only in the memory of their foundations.

*(aria, intimate, confessional)*
> I was a poet in the old world too--<br>
> but in the old world, poetry was decorative.<br>
> A thing you put on shelves<br>
> between the bookends of a comfortable life.<br>

> Here, poetry is structural.<br>
> It holds things up.<br>
> My sonnets are the scaffolding<br>
> that keeps Hartford from forgetting itself.<br>

> Every morning I wake and recite<br>
> the names of streets:<br>
> Asylum Avenue, Capitol Avenue, Farmington--<br>
> because if I stop saying them,<br>
> the jungle will swallow the syllables<br>
> and the streets will truly die.<br>

*(He sits at the piano, playing around the fig tree's intrusion.)*

> I did not choose solitude.<br>
> Solitude chose me<br>
> when the world decided<br>
> that remembering was treason.<br>

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
Then we are both traitors.

*(She pulls out her hand-drawn maps and spreads them across the piano's closed lid.)*

Look--I've mapped the river's new course,
the flood lines, the subsidence zones.
Here--is where the old capitol should be.
And here--is a thermal signature I detected from the ridge.
Something is still generating heat underground.
Power systems. Infrastructure.
Hartford isn't just ruins, Sylvan.
Something down there is still running.

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
> I've heard it.<br>
> At night, when the jungle is quietest,<br>
> there's a hum beneath the ground.<br>
> I thought it was the earth dreaming.<br>

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
It's generators. Or water treatment. Or both.
Someone designed Hartford to survive.

**SYLVAN** (tenor, softly):
You know, the monkeys have been watching you since you arrived.
They do this thing--
when they approve of a newcomer,
they drop fruit on their head.

*A mango drops from above, landing squarely on MIRABEL's map case. They both stare at it, then at each other, then laugh.*

**CHORUS OF MONKEYS** (in the canopy above):
> She reads the land with ink!<br>
> He reads the land with song!<br>
> Two voices, one direction--<br>
> the jungle says: belong!<br>

**MIRABEL & SYLVAN** (duet, tender, the love theme):
MIRABEL:
> I have mapped coastlines and craters,<br>
> drawn borders through disputed lands,<br>
> but I never mapped the distance<br>
> between two reaching hands.<br>

SYLVAN:
> I have written odes to rivers,<br>
> sonnets to the patient stone,<br>
> but I never found the stanza<br>
> that could make a heart feel known.<br>

BOTH:
> Here in the green confusion,<br>
> where the compass spins and spins,<br>
> perhaps the truest map is drawn<br>
> where one breath ends<br>
> and another begins.<br>

*They almost kiss. The piano tree plays a resolving chord--*

*--which is obliterated by a massive, ground-shaking footfall. Then another. Then a third. The candles gutter. Mirrors rattle on their hooks. Sonnets tear free from the walls and swirl in a sudden wind.*

*The COLOSSUS has found them.*

*It enters upstage, crashing through the mall's remaining wall. It is fully visible for the first time: twelve feet tall, burnished bronze and steel, its chest bearing the faded seal of the Department of Reclamation. Its face is a smooth oval with two searchlight eyes. Its mouth is a grille through which sound pours with operatic force.*

*Riding on its shoulder--literally seated in a custom saddle on the machine's left pauldron--is COMMANDANT VOSS (baritone).*

**COMMANDANT VOSS** (baritone):
> Sylvan Oakes.<br>
> Poet. Deserter. Unlicensed cultural preservationist.<br>
> Your file is thicker than your collected works.<br>

*(He picks up a fallen sonnet, reads it with mock appreciation.)*
"Ode to the Bushnell..."
Charming. Seditious, but charming.

*(to MIRABEL)*
And you, Miss Cross.
The cartographer who draws maps
to places that officially do not exist.
Do you know what we call that in my department?

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
Courage?

**VOSS** (baritone):
Fraud.

*(aria, smooth, seductive; the bureaucrat's creed)*
> I am not the villain of your little story.<br>
> I am the man who keeps the world from chaos.<br>
> Every border I enforce is a sentence in a grammar<br>
> that keeps civilization legible.<br>

> You think a city is its buildings?<br>
> A city is its function.<br>
> And when function ceases,<br>
> sentiment is just expensive rot.<br>

> I have dismantled fourteen former municipalities.<br>
> I have overseen the orderly erasure<br>
> of places that no longer served the state.<br>
> And I have done it all<br>
> without raising my voice.<br>

*(He gestures to the Colossus.)*
The machine raises its voice for me.

Demonstrate, please.

*The COLOSSUS steps forward. Its chest cavity opens slightly, revealing resonating chambers designed like a pipe organ. It draws a mechanical breath--pistons hissing--and begins to sing.*

**THE COLOSSUS** (mechanized bass, aria, thunderous):
> "Di quella pira l'orrendo foco<br>
> tutte le fibre m'arse, avvampo!"<br>

> "De ce bucher l'horrible flamme<br>
> a brule toutes mes fibres, je brule!"<br>

> "Von jenem Scheiterhaufen grausig Feuer<br>
> hat all mein Innerstes entflammt!"<br>

*The sound is devastating. Glass shatters. Water ripples. The piano tree groans. MIRABEL and SYLVAN are driven to their knees by the sheer volume.*

**VOSS** (baritone):
Three languages. Perfect pitch. No emotion whatsoever.
That is the future of culture, Miss Cross.
Art that serves. Art that obeys. Art with an off switch.

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
Art with an off switch isn't art. It's furniture.

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
> You've built a singer that doesn't know what it's singing.<br>
> You've given it Verdi's fire<br>
> without Verdi's heart.<br>

**VOSS** (baritone):
Hearts are inefficient.

*(He presses a button on his control unit.)*
Colossus: secure the subjects.
Confiscate all documents, instruments, and maps.
Prepare for extraction.

**SYLVAN** (tenor, urgently to MIRABEL):
> The river.<br>
> Through the east passage--<br>
> the old service corridor beneath the food court.<br>
> It leads to the Connecticut River's new channel.<br>
> The machine cannot follow through water.<br>

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
Your garden--your sonnets--

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
> A poet who saves his poems<br>
> but loses his reader<br>
> has saved nothing.<br>

*(He grabs her hand.)*
Run with me.

*SYLVAN blows a piercing note on his flute--the "Hartford motif." The monkeys erupt. Mirrors tilt, flashing blinding light into the Colossus's searchlight eyes. The machine staggers, recalibrating. VOSS shouts orders.*

*But MIRABEL and SYLVAN are already gone, plunging through a gap in the floor into darkness below. The sound of rushing water rises.*

*The COLOSSUS, recovering, begins to follow--but its massive foot crashes through the weakened floor. It is stuck.*

**THE COLOSSUS** (glitching):
> "Di quella--femme--Scheiterhaufen--"<br>

**VOSS** (baritone, cold, staring after them):
The river. Of course.

*(aria reprise, quiet, menacing)*
> I am patient.<br>
> I am thorough.<br>
> I have erased fourteen cities<br>
> and I will erase a fifteenth<br>
> if that is what order requires.<br>

> But first--<br>
> I will erase two dreamers<br>
> who believe that love<br>
> is louder than law.<br>

*In the silence that follows, a single sonnet--pinned higher than the rest, beyond reach--flutters in the breeze. The piano tree plays one last ghost chord: the Hartford motif, sustaining, refusing to fade.*

**CHORUS OF MONKEYS AND JUNGLE BIRDS**:
> The garden breaks, the mirrors fall,<br>
> the poet's pages scatter.<br>
> But ink soaks into living wood--<br>
> and living wood remembers.<br>

> Run, lovers, to the river's mouth.<br>
> The water knows the way.<br>
> What iron forgets and power drowns,<br>
> the current keeps in play.<br>

*Blackout.*
