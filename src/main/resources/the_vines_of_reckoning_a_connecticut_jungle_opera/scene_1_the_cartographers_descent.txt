Scene 1: The Cartographer's Descent
Author: GPT-5.2

*The curtain rises on a wall of green--impenetrable, primordial. What was once the Merritt Parkway is now a canyon of strangler figs and ceiba trees, their roots cracking through asphalt like slow-motion lightning. Rusted guardrails arc upward, seized by kudzu, forming natural archways. A faded green highway sign, barely legible, reads: HARTFORD -- EXIT 27. Below it, a newer sign--hand-painted on bark--reads: TURN BACK. NOTHING LEFT.*

*Morning light filters through the canopy in cathedral shafts. The air hums with cicadas, dripping moisture, and the distant shriek of macaws--species that should not exist this far north, but the climate has rewritten the rules.*

*From stage right, a rope drops from the canopy. Down it slides MIRABEL CROSS (soprano), the cartographer-explorer--wiry, sunburned, fierce. She wears a battered field vest covered in hand-stitched pockets, each containing instruments: a sextant, a barometer, a hand-cranked radio. On her back, a leather map case. Her boots hit the ground and she crouches, listening.*

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
> Twenty-seven days south from the New Haven perimeter.<br>
> Fourteen rivers forded. Three bridges collapsed beneath my weight.<br>
> The satellite maps show nothing here but green--<br>
> the government's favorite color for erasure.<br>

*(She pulls out a hand-drawn map, comparing it to the terrain.)*
> But the old maps don't lie.<br>
> The Connecticut River still bends here,<br>
> still carries the memory of a city in its current.<br>

*(aria, determined, ascending)*
> I am the cartographer of forbidden places,<br>
> the woman who draws what power denies.<br>
> Every blank space on every sanctioned map<br>
> is a confession--<br>
> a government admitting what it fears.<br>

> Hartford, I have followed your ghost<br>
> through swamp and canopy,<br>
> through checkpoints and lies,<br>
> through nights when the only light<br>
> was the phosphor of my own stubborn belief.<br>

> They erased you from the records.<br>
> I will draw you back into existence.<br>

*She kneels, pressing her palm to the cracked asphalt. She closes her eyes. The jungle noise dims--and beneath it, almost imagined, comes a low pulse: the heartbeat of buried infrastructure, of water still flowing through pipes no one maintains.*

*From the canopy above, a shower of crumpled paper falls--not leaves, but pages. Handwritten sonnets on bark-paper, waterlogged and beautiful. MIRABEL catches one, reads it with growing astonishment.*

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
> "O Hartford, your insurance towers dream<br>
> beneath the figs, your actuarial tables<br>
> have become the jungle's inventory--"<br>

*(She looks up.)*
Who writes poetry to a dead city?

*A figure drops silently from a branch above--SYLVAN OAKES (tenor), the jungle poet. He is barefoot, lean as a vine, dressed in layers of salvaged fabric dyed green and brown with plant pigments. His hair is long, braided with small feathers. He carries a bamboo writing case and a hand-carved wooden flute. He lands with the ease of someone who has lived among branches for years.*

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
> I write poetry to a living city.<br>
> The dead don't need sonnets.<br>
> But a city buried alive--<br>
> that needs every word I can give it.<br>

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
You're the poet. The one the smugglers mentioned.
"Mad Oakes," they called you.
"Talks to buildings. Serenades the ruins."

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
> I prefer "composer-in-residence<br>
> of the Connecticut Reclamation Zone."<br>
> The monkeys are my orchestra.<br>
> The trees are my concert hall.<br>
> The audience is... usually just me.<br>

*(He takes back the fallen sonnet, tenderly.)*
Until today.

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
I'm not here for poetry. I'm here for Hartford.

**SYLVAN** (tenor, aria, lyrical, earnest):
> Then you are here for poetry.<br>
> Because Hartford is a poem now--<br>
> not a place you find on any grid,<br>
> but a rhythm you must learn to hear.<br>

> I have walked its drowned avenues<br>
> by moonlight, knee-deep in warm water,<br>
> and I have heard the buildings hum--<br>
> not with electricity, but with remembrance.<br>

> The old Travelers Tower still stands,<br>
> leaning like an ancient singer<br>
> who refuses to leave the stage.<br>
> At dawn, the vines that grip its sides<br>
> vibrate in the wind like strings.<br>

*(He plays a brief, haunting phrase on his flute--the "Hartford motif.")*

> That is Hartford's voice.<br>
> I didn't compose it.<br>
> I only learned to listen.<br>

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
You know the way.

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
> I know many ways.<br>
> But the jungle changes its mind daily.<br>
> Paths that existed yesterday are swallowed by morning.<br>
> You need someone who speaks the green language.<br>

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
And you need someone with maps.

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
I need someone who believes
a city can be resurrected with ink.

**MIRABEL & SYLVAN** (duet, tentative, then building):
MIRABEL: I draw lines through chaos--
SYLVAN: I hear music in the wreckage--
BOTH: And somewhere in the green cathedral, a city waits for witnesses.

*Their voices blend for the first time. The jungle responds--birdsong shifting key, monkeys chattering in something almost like approval.*

*But the moment is shattered. From far off, a sound unlike anything natural: a deep, resonant, mechanized voice singing the opening of Verdi's "La donna e mobile"--in Italian, perfect, terrifying.*

**THE COLOSSUS** (offstage, mechanized bass):
> "La donna e mobile<br>
> qual piuma al vento..."<br>

*The jungle goes silent. Even the cicadas stop.*

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
> Run.<br>
> That is not a bird.<br>
> That is not a storm.<br>
> That is the government's throat.<br>

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
What is it?

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
They call it the Colossus.
A walking opera house with fists.
It sings Verdi while it searches.
And behind it--

**COMMANDANT VOSS** (baritone, offstage, amplified):
Attention, unauthorized personnel.
You are in violation of Federal Reclamation Statute 47-B.
The territory designated "Former Hartford Metropolitan Area"
is classified, sealed, and forbidden.

Miss Cross. I have read your grant applications.
Fascinating fiction.
Please surrender your instruments and maps.
The Colossus will escort you to processing.

**THE COLOSSUS** (offstage, now in French):
> "La femme est volage<br>
> comme plume au vent..."<br>

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
Processing. That's what they call it when they erase you.

**SYLVAN** (tenor):
> We go deeper.<br>
> The jungle is my ally. The Colossus is heavy.<br>
> Vines will slow it. Water will confuse it.<br>
> But we must move now.<br>

*(He extends his hand to her.)*
Do you trust a mad poet?

**MIRABEL** (soprano):
I trust anyone the government wants silenced.

*They plunge into the undergrowth together, vanishing into the green. Behind them, the Colossus's Verdi swells--now in English:*

**THE COLOSSUS** (offstage, growing louder):
> "Woman is fickle<br>
> as a feather in the wind..."<br>

**CHORUS OF MONKEYS AND JUNGLE BIRDS**:
> Run, run, through root and river!<br>
> The iron throat is coming!<br>
> But the jungle has ten thousand doors<br>
> and the government has only one key!<br>

*The stage darkens except for the fading glow of fireflies and the cold, sweeping beam of the Colossus's searchlight, probing the canopy like a mechanical eye.*

*Blackout.*
